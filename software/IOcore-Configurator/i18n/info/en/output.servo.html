Setting up a servo

A servo turns to an angle between 0° and 180°. As a turnout motor it moves between two positions:

  • Position 0 (curved): the angle for "curved".
  • Position 1 (straight): the angle for "straight".
  • Speed: milliseconds per degree. 20 ms/° is calm and realistic; 5 is fast; 50 is very slow.

Finding the right positions

  1. With the board connected, tick Live and drag the Position 0 slider slowly. The servo moves along. Stop when the turnout blade just touches the rail, then do the same for Position 1. It is stored at once.
  2. Or without a computer: use the config button and loco address 9999 on your throttle (see Help → Config button).

Tip: do not push the blade too hard against the rail: the servo will hum and get warm. Go back 2–3° from the point where it touches.

Semaphore arm: following a detector

Tick Follows a detector (semaphore arm) and choose the detector input: the board then moves the servo itself, without commands. When that block is occupied the servo goes to Position 0 (curved) = the arm at stop; when it is free, to Position 1 (straight) = clear. It moves at its own speed. Swap: occupied = straight turns this round. Any input that is in use and has a pin can be the detector, best a Sensor (occupancy) input; on the canvas a dotted line links it to the servo.

Good to know

  • At power-up the servos start one by one, half a second apart, each to its last position. That avoids a big current peak.
  • At most 6 servos move at the same time; the others wait for their turn.
  • Wiring: brown/black = GND, red = 5 V (from a separate 5 V supply for several servos!), orange/yellow = signal.
