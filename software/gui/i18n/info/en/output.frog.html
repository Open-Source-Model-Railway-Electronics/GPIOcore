Frog polarity — electrofrog or unifrog?

The frog is the point of a turnout where the two rails cross. On “live frog” turnouts this piece is metal and must get the same polarity as the route that is set. If it gets the wrong one, locos stall or cause a short circuit. A relay switches the frog when the turnout moves.

Look at your turnout and choose one:

  • None (dead frog) — the frog is plastic (Peco Insulfrog, most set-track). Nothing to do.
  • Electrofrog — 2 relays (Peco Electrofrog and similar). Relay A is switched on for “straight”, relay B for “curved”. While the turnout is moving both are off for a moment, so there is never a short circuit (break before make).
  • Unifrog — 1 relay (Peco Unifrog). The frog has one wire. One change-over relay connects it to the correct rail. With a servo it switches exactly when the servo passes the middle; with a coil motor at the end of the pulse.

You can only use one of the two. Choosing one removes the pins of the other.

Wiring: use a relay module (with transistor and diode) — an Arduino pin cannot drive a bare relay.
