Automatic block signal

The board sets this signal by itself from occupancy detectors, without a command station:

  • red when its own block is occupied,
  • yellow when its own block is free but the next block is occupied,
  • green when both are free.

A block is “occupied” while its input is active (pressed = LOW): make that input a Sensor (occupancy), for example a current detector, a reed contact or a light barrier, on a digital pin or as an analog condition. The board checks it every 50 ms.

Choose which lamp (A–D) is red, yellow and green. The signal shows one lamp at a time; the flashing frequency is not used. Leave Next block at “none” for a signal with only red and green.

Several signals in a row: signal 1 protects block 1 and looks at block 2, signal 2 protects block 2 and looks at block 3, and so on. A train in block 2 then gives signal 2 red and signal 1 yellow.

Block on the next board: the next block’s detector may be on another board. Wire its signal through an optocoupler to an input here, for example D0 (RX): D0 is free once no PC has connected within 5 s after power-up.

Switched off, the signal is a normal signal again: the aspect comes from DCC or a route.
