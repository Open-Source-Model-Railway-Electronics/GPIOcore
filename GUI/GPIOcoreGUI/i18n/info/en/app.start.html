Getting started

This program sets up a GPIOcore board: a small Arduino (Nano / Uno, or a DCCnext) that sits under your layout and switches things: turnouts, lights, signals, relays. You tell the board what is connected to which pin, and what should make it move.

The two kinds of things

  • Outputs (right column) are things the board does: move a servo, pulse a turnout coil, light an LED, switch a relay. There are 16 of them.
  • Inputs (left column) are things that tell the board to do something: a push button on your control panel, or a command from your DCC command station (your digital system / handheld). There are 16 of them.

Step by step

  1. Connect. Plug the board into the USB port. Choose its port at the top (it usually says CH340, USB Serial or Arduino) and click Connect. No board at hand? Click Demo to try everything with a pretend board.
  2. Set up an output. Click an output on the right. A panel slides in from the right. Choose the type (for example Servo) and the pin where the wire goes.
  3. Set up an input. Click an input on the left. A panel slides in from the left. Choose what starts it: a button pin, a DCC address, or both.
  4. Connect them. Drag the output (right) onto the input (left). A coloured line appears: that input now operates that output. The output gets the colour of its input, so you can see at a glance what belongs together.
  5. Send it to the board. Everything you changed gets an orange dot. Click Write … change(s) at the top. The board stores it, even when the power is off.

Good to know

  • Every (i) button explains the thing next to it. Click it whenever you are unsure.
  • Red text means something is wrong, for example two things on the same pin.
  • Test state 0 / 1 in an output panel makes it move right away, so you can check your wiring.
  • File → Save keeps a copy of your setup on the computer. Do this before you experiment.
  • Made a mess? Revert throws away everything you have not written to the board yet.
